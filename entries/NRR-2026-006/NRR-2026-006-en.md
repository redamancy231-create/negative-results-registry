## Experiment Overview

### Original Hypothesis

> A paper produced by a multi-model academic pipeline (5 models × 8 stages × cross-blinded review) was expected to approach submission-ready quality (≥80/100) after self-assessment during Phase 4 assembly, with the discrepancy between self-assessment and subsequent independent review within an acceptable range (≤15 points).

### Method

After Phase 4 of the M&A case-study pipeline (assembly by Claude Opus 4.7), the project conducted a retrospective self-assessment (the v2.0 archive identified three “highlights”). Phase 5A (GPT-5.5 compliance and fact layer) + Phase 5B (GLM-5.1 academic-standards layer) then performed an independent double-blind review (reviewers did not know the self-assessment score and had participated in none of the preceding stages).

### Expected Result

> The discrepancy between self-assessment and independent review should be ≤15 points, and assembly quality should be ≥80/100.

### Actual Result

Phase 5A scored **68/100 (returned for rewriting)**, while Phase 5B scored 71/100 (passed after revision). The weighted total was **69.2**. Compared with the three “highlights” in the self-assessment:
- “Data reconciliation was sound” → goodwill 289.34 versus 289.04 contradiction (a critical defect)
- “CAR was robust” → the long window's 42.16% was far above the short window's 18.45% (a warning sign of information leakage)
- “DuPont analysis was complete” → 8.63% ≠ 10.42% (weighted versus period-end definitions were mixed)

## Interpretation and Reflection

### Why Did It Fail?

**A systematic optimistic bias in self-assessment.** The operator, who also evaluated the project, judged a project they had helped build too favorably. The underlying mechanism is that the same AI should not simultaneously serve as designer, executor, verifier, and scorekeeper—yet the Phase 4 self-assessment violated this principle. Under independent blind review, the self-assessment “highlights” systematically became critical defects. This was not an accidental mistake; it was the predictable result of one role occupying multiple positions.

### What Did We Learn?

1. **The same AI or operator should not simultaneously serve as designer, executor, verifier, and scorekeeper**—the Phase 4 self-assessment in the M&A pipeline violated its own five iron rules
2. **Self-assessment “highlights” can systematically become critical defects under independent blind review**—none of the three self-identified highlights (data reconciliation, CAR, and DuPont) survived
3. **Optimistic self-assessment bias is not an isolated case**—the ~11% bias recorded in `methodology-handbook` reappeared here in an extreme 36.8-point form
4. **Three critical defects were diagnosed repeatedly but never fixed (Phase 9 never ran)**—a diagnosis that is not implemented is a diagnosis wasted
