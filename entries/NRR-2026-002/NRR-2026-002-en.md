## Experiment Overview

### Original Hypothesis

> Declarative action routing (defining available actions in YAML/JSON → exact matching) was expected to outperform natural-language routing (describing available actions in natural language → semantic matching), because the declarative format reduces room for ambiguous LLM interpretation of action semantics.

### Method

Prompt-TDD controlled experiment design (pilot stage):
- **Experimental group**: declarative action-routing prompt (actions defined in YAML, strict matching)
- **Control group**: natural-language routing prompt (actions described in natural language, semantic matching)
- **Task**: a Claude Code agent autonomously selected actions during multi-step tasks (file operations, search, execution, and so on)
- **Sample size**: 15 pilot cases (experimental-design stage—Tier 0; did not proceed to Tier 1 inferential statistics)
- **Evaluation**: routing accuracy + task-completion rate

### Expected Result

> Declarative routing should reduce action-selection errors, with an expected improvement in routing accuracy of ≥10%.

### Actual Result

Across the 15 pilot cases, the two groups showed no significant difference in routing accuracy or task-completion rate. The effect size was insufficient to justify proceeding to a full-scale Tier 1 experiment.

## Interpretation and Reflection

### Why Did It Fail?

Two possible explanations:
1. **The LLM was already a sufficiently capable semantic router**—when actions were described in natural language, the LLM's semantic understanding was sufficient to resolve ambiguity, making the extra precision of the declarative format redundant
2. **The pilot sample was too small**—15 cases may have been insufficient to detect a small effect. But the purpose of the pilot was precisely to determine at low cost whether a full-scale experiment was worth the investment; the answer was “no”

### What Did We Learn?

1. **The intuition that “format equals precision” is only partly correct**—declarative formats outperform natural language in conventional programming, but an LLM acting as a semantic engine may process natural language more naturally
2. **A pilot's value lies in stopping losses early**—15 cases were enough to decide against Tier 1, saving approximately 4 hours of experiment execution and 2 hours of review time
3. **A negative pilot is still a success**—A3 was labeled in the methodology handbook as a **counterexample case** showing how to close an experiment with no signal; this teaching value is more distinctive than a positive result
4. **Consistency with A2 is noteworthy but does not establish a domain-level conclusion**—A2 (n=24 controlled) and A3 (n=15 pilot) each detected no difference under their respective conditions, creating a meta-hypothesis for future testing; an aggregate study would require preregistration, multiple tasks, and adequate power before drawing a general conclusion
