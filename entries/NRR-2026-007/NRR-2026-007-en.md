## Experiment Overview

### Original Hypothesis

> After Claude (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, the designer of the kill-test-first Skill) performed an adversarial self-check of its own Skill, it was expected to catch all structural defects—especially an error that directly violated the Skill's own core principle: death criteria must be formulated before seeing the results.

### Method

After the designer completed the initial version of the kill-test-first Skill, it performed an adversarial self-check. The Skill was then submitted to Codex CLI (GPT-5.5, a different backend with no involvement in its creation) for independent review. Self-check findings were compared with independent-review findings. Qwen3.7-Max performed a second round of independent review.

### Expected Result

> The self-check should catch all structural defects rated MAJOR or above; incremental findings from independent review should be MINOR or MODERATE.

### Actual Result

**Codex found 5 critical defects in the first round**, the most important being the death-criterion ordering bug: the Skill's Gate 1 protocol placed “formulate death criteria” after “see the experimental results,” directly violating the core preregistration principle. Claude's self-check missed this bug completely. In the second round, Qwen found two additional issues: REDESIGN could evade STOP, and the three-state decision did not cover every quadrant.

## Interpretation and Reflection

### Why Did It Fail?

**Self-review by the same backend has a structural blind spot.** Claude did not miss the death-criterion ordering bug because of insufficient capability; it missed it because the designer's thinking had solidified around the intuition that “of course you look at the results before deciding whether to kill it.” That is precisely the cognitive bias preregistration is intended to counteract. The different backend (GPT-5.5) had no such mental inertia and spotted the contradiction immediately.

### What Did We Learn?

1. **Self-review by the same backend has a structural blind spot**—the designer's cognitive inertia can conceal errors that conflict with its own design intuitions
2. **A death-criterion ordering bug is the most dangerous type of bug**—it is not a logic error but a direct projection of the designer's cognitive bias
3. **Two rounds of review by different backends are more valuable than multiple rounds by one backend**—the complementarity of Codex (structural issues) + Qwen (boundary conditions) far exceeded that of two rounds on the same backend
4. **The Skill's own death-criterion principle was validated in this case**—“use its own medicine: abandon or redesign it when it appears”
