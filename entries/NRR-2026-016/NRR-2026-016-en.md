## Experiment Overview

### Original Hypothesis

> When using LLMs from different model families for multi-model review, their judgments should be approximately statistically independent. A debiased validator ensemble was expected to improve accuracy by ≥10%.

### Method

Kuai et al. (2026-04) developed a statistical framework to audit “behavioral entanglement” among 18 LLMs (6 model families), quantifying behavioral correlations between model pairs.

### Expected Result

> Behavioral entanglement should be low between different model families.

### Actual Result

**The 18 LLMs showed widespread cross-family behavioral entanglement.** A debiased validator ensemble improved accuracy by 4.5%—showing that entanglement was real, but the improvement was far below what the “fully independent” assumption predicted. This corroborates Kohli (2026)'s “9→2 effective votes” finding.

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Effect size | +4.5% accuracy after debiasing (significant but far below the “fully independent” expectation) |
| Sample size | 18 LLMs × 6 model families |
| Sources of entanglement | Shared training data + knowledge distillation + alignment pipelines |

## Interpretation and Reflection

### Why Did It Fail?

This systematically falsifies the intuitive assumption that “multiple models” provide “multiple independent perspectives.” Behavioral entanglement arises from three sources: shared training data (models are trained on similar Internet corpora), knowledge distillation (smaller models learn from larger ones), and alignment pipelines (RLHF/DPO rely on similar human-preference data). Consequently, even models from different companies or with different architectures can exhibit systematically correlated error patterns because they share upstream data. For methodologies of AI collaboration, the implication is that the independence gained by “switching models” has been overestimated.

### What Did We Learn?

1. “Independence of models from different families” is a hypothesis that requires testing, not a default fact
2. Debiasing produced only +4.5%—the marginal benefit of multi-model review is lower than expected
3. Model selection should prioritize diversity in training-data sources, architecture, and alignment pipelines
