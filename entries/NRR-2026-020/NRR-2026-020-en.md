## Experiment Overview

### Original Hypothesis

> After three independent analyses of PocketFlow (a 100-line core plus a cookbook organized by difficulty), at least three reusable methodology patterns were expected to reach the [E] trial level.

### Method

Three independent analyses (DeepSeek-V4-Pro + GPT-5.5 + Kimi-K2.7) extracted three asset groups: B1 minimal core + example add-ons, B2 difficulty stratification, and C1-C3 anti-pattern list. Each was assessed against the framework's evidence-grade criteria.

### Expected Result

> At least B1 should reach the [E] trial level—supported by ≥1 independent source project and not merely speculative.

### Actual Result

**All 3/3 asset groups were [Sp] speculative level.** B1's N=2 evidence provided only directional indication. Extracting methodology from a 100-line core project has an inherent information-density ceiling.

## Interpretation and Reflection

### Why Did It Fail?

This was not a problem with PocketFlow—minimalist design is itself a methodological position. The problem was insufficient information density → more inference/extrapolation → evidence grade could only remain at [Sp]. This ceiling was not caused by insufficient analytical depth; it was imposed by the information capacity of the source project itself.

### What Did We Learn?

1. Methodology extraction from minimal projects has an inherent ceiling—the smaller the source project, the harder it is for the evidence grade to exceed [Sp]
2. N=2 evidence has only directional-indication value—it cannot carry the weight of [E]-level evidence
3. A 100-line core is both a methodological strength and an extraction limitation—it cannot provide evidence of diversity
4. For small external projects, set an evidence-grade ceiling in advance—avoid discovering only after three analysis rounds that everything is [Sp]
