## Experiment Overview

### Original Hypothesis

> The version-upgrade protocol in framework §2.6 was expected to be followed strictly during the framework's own version evolution.

### Method

Codex (GPT-5.5) performed a cross-version review of v1.6, checking item by item whether the §2.6 review requirements had been met during the v1.5→v1.6 upgrade.

### Expected Result

> The v1.6 upgrade should satisfy independent review by ≥2 backends.

### Actual Result

§2.6 requires ≥2-backend review for a Minor upgrade—but **v1.6 (v1.5→v1.6 was a Minor upgrade) was completed using only one backend.** “The doctor's child goes untreated.”

## Interpretation and Reflection

### Why Did It Fail?

This is a direct instance of the “physician, heal thyself” problem. The framework devotes substantial attention to establishing the necessity of independent, multi-backend review and encodes that requirement as a hard constraint in §2.6; yet the constraint was silently bypassed during an upgrade of the framework itself. The issue was not a lack of capacity—the project already had the infrastructure and procedures for multi-backend review—but rather that the operator was simultaneously the designer and executor of the review process, leaving no external enforcement mechanism. This is a direct manifestation of limitation #9 in §1.8: author–reader isomorphism.

### What Did We Learn?

1. “The doctor's child goes untreated”—rule makers are often the first to violate their own rules
2. The existence of a rule ≠ enforcement of the rule—a rule system without independent auditing has structural vulnerabilities
3. A transition clause is an honest repair: “We require others to do this, but we are still building the infrastructure to do it ourselves”
