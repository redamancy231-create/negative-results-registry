## Experiment Overview

### Original Hypothesis

> Systematically extract methodology patterns for AI collaboration from 22 projects (11 external analyses, 7 self-built public projects, and 4 self-built nonpublic projects). At least 5–8 stable patterns were expected to appear across ≥3 source projects and form “Methodology Extraction Framework v0.1.”

### Method

Eight-stage systematic extraction process (Phase 0-7):
- **Source projects**: 22 (11 external open-source/paper analyses + 11 self-built projects)
- **Extraction method**: create an independent evidence card for each project → cross-project clustering → pattern candidates → G5 traceability audit
- **Stability threshold**: independently present in ≥3 source projects
- **Review**: 10 review rounds across four backends—GPT-5.5 / Kimi-K2.7 / Qwen3.7-Max / GLM-5.2
- **Meta-review**: Kohli (2026-05) independence framework → approximately 2–3 effectively independent perspectives

### Expected Result

> Five to eight methodology patterns would meet the ≥3-source stability threshold, producing a publishable “Methodology Extraction Framework v0.1.”

### Actual Result

**G5 traceability audit conclusion: none of the five components met the ≥3-source stability threshold.** The 22 source projects were insufficient to support extraction of generalizable methodology patterns.

## Interpretation and Reflection

### Why Did It Fail?

Three systemic problems compounded one another:
1. **Sample homogeneity**: the same operator extracted and reviewed every project. Although the source projects spanned different domains (quantitative finance, code review, and document generation), their level of methodological self-awareness was highly correlated through the operator; there was no independent “control sample”
2. **n=22 was too small**: extracting cross-project patterns requires a sample far larger than 22, especially when projects differ substantially (some contain tens of thousands of lines of code; others are documentation only)
3. **The stability threshold was too strict**: ≥3 sources may be too demanding for methodology-literature extraction; cross-study pattern extraction in academic literature commonly requires 30–50 papers

### What Did We Learn?

1. **Methodology extraction is an order of magnitude harder than expected**—22 projects looks substantial but is nowhere near enough
2. **Operator independence is the bottleneck**—when one person extracts every source project, that person's cognitive preferences systematically constrain pattern discovery
3. **“Could not extract” and “does not exist” are different claims**—zero qualifying patterns from 22 projects does not mean no extractable patterns exist; the method may be inadequate
4. **The process was more valuable than the result**—the review chain (33 nonoverlapping findings across two rounds plus evidence of convergence across four backends) had greater scholarly value than the framework's output
5. **An honest negative result has independent value**—the project's central deliverable shifted from a “methodology extraction framework” to an “honest report on how difficult this problem is”
